Józef Chełmoński

While looking at pieces for another post I kept looking at art from Józef Chełmoński. While I didn't use any of them I loved them so much I'm putting them here.
This is not a summary of "Chełmoński's Best Works" or his "Most Influential Works". This is just what personally connected with me. I'm sure there's some very notable works I didn't include and some obscure ones that don't make sense to have, that's not what this list is for.

For the record, I do kinda know how to saw his name. In Polish "ch" is prnounced "heh" and the ł is like a "W".

This piece—wow. This was the first one I saw and it's what set me down this rabbit-hole. I actually wanted want to rewrite the crucifixion scene to be more cold and lonely, but I'm glad I didn't. When I see this painting I don't think about Christ necessarily, I think of the simple rural faith in Eastern Europe.
Chełmoński's Wikipedia calls him a "realist" painter; yeah that's what stands out to me. This painting is so real.

Most of Chełmoński's work is people with horses. While these aren't my favorite works of his, these specifically stood out to me. I love the soft face of the daughter in the back of the sleigh. This is such a "Christmas" image to me.

Eastern Europe loves the Cossacks. Historically they were a civilian militia; the rulers didn't defend them so they defended themselves. And they were extremely successfull, the Turks greatly feared them and the people loved them. My understanding is that today they are a symbol of freedom and heavily romanticized.

According to wikipedia, toward the end of his life Chełmoński painted people less and less, but painted nature more and more. The lighting on this one is just beautiful, that moonlight is just the perfect shade. Oh to be a hunter with his dogs and horses in the snow. These pieces remind me that this was how these people worked. I assume they hunted, and would carry their kills through the snow back to their homes/market. The sleighs riding into untouched snow evoke not forntiersman per say, but certainly people who lived on the edge of dangerous nature.

Now we are getting to the truly great stuff, the pieces focused entirely on nature. It seems he was semi-retired at this point and could've painted anything, but this is what he chose to make, and it's beautiful. This meadow is not a cleanly designed gardens but mostly wild grass and flowers. There aren't exciting horses or people all posed around, this is what captivated Chełmoński at this time; the simple nature he saw daily.
The distant trees actually remind me of grain silos and barns from my hometown.

[credit line here]

This is my absolute favorite. The fog in the morning, the silhouetted cranes, the dots of red flowers, it moves something in me. They remind me of waking up early at a quiet lakefront cottage, sitting on the porch drinking coffee with no one else around.
