Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám, the Astronomer-Poet of Persia

Elihu Vedder's most famous work may be his illustrations for Edward FitzGerald's translation of Omar Khayyám's poems.
This may be controversial, but I found Vedder's art really elevated the words on the page. Every page turn gave me something new and wonderful I didn't expect.
For anyone else who also finds the text hard to read, and The Internet Archive's scans low quality, I made this page that uses the high quality scans from the Smithsonian with an overlay over the text boxes. I've added the page turning to keep the "surprise with every page".
While working on this it's possible I'm the first person in history ever to proofread a transcription of this text while inside a Taco Bell.
Note: each of these 55 four-line poems was originally written in Persian as totally self-contained poems. When FitzGerald translated them he purposely changed the wording and placed the poems in a specific order to tell one continuous story.
