Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám, the Astronomer-Poet of Persia

Elihu Vedder's most famous work may be his illustrations for Edward FitzGerald's translation of Omar Khayyám's poems. I found Vedder's art really elevated the words on the page, every page turn gave me something wonderful I didn't expect. However, the Internet Archive's scan of the book was very low quality, and the Smithsonian does put their higher quality scans in order. So, I threw this together with a text overlay for those who find the text hard to read. While working on this it's possible I'm the first person in history ever to proofread a transcription of this text while inside a Taco Bell.
Note: These 100 four-line stanzas were not originally written as one large work, but 100 small self-contained poems. Edward Fitzgerald, half a world away and 700 years later, rearanged the poems to create the narrative of a single long day. He only included poems he could make fit and discarded ones he could not, and drastically changing words/sentence structure to make it happen. Different editions of this book would even swap out entire stanzas for a different poem he thought fit better.
I still believe it's worth reading, just realize this is not what you'd call a good-faith translation.
