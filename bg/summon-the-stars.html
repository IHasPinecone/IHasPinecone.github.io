Summon the Stars: A Coop Variant for Wingspan

(yeah you read that right)

Summon the Stars is an experimental board game Rorschach test. All players are working together to win, but the game has mulitple win conditions, and the win condition achieved will reveal conscious/unconscious biases of the players.

Players will play a mostly standard game of Wingspan, based on how many points they score as a group and whether they balance the humors (more on that later) they will achieve one of 4 endings. Endings both have immediate rewards (such as satisfaction for beating a difficult challenge and signing the board) and legacy rewards (new content/character upgrades).

Setup

To play you will need a standard copy of Wingspan, a printout of the humors board, X pieces to track the clock, and a character sheet for each player.

Setup a standard game for 2-5 players.
Do not deal out starting bonus cards (although players may gain bonus cards through other means)
Do not setup the objectives board. (Players may still use the objective board to count how many rounds have passed.)
Players shall choose a difficulty level between 1 and 10. New players should start on difficulty 1, more experienced players may start on difficulty 2 or 3. As characters level up they will be able to beat higher difficulty levels. Multiply difficulty by the player count, and place the clock pawn on that number.
If this is the first game, players shall choose one of the starting character sheets and sign it to claim it as theirs. Players will take starting birds indicated on their character sheet, plus an additional 3 random birds from the deck.

Play

Players will take their turns simultaneously, in any order they wish. Players may wish to sequence their turns to fully utilize birds that give resources to all players.
Each player begins the round with 7 action cubes. Leave played cubes sitting in the habitat as in the normal game, they will be used in the humors step.

During the humors step all cubes in habitats must be removed to affect the humors (cubes used to play birds do not affect the humors). Determine the direction and magnitude of imbalance of cubes between the habitat by removing one cube from every habitat until one habitat has no cubes.
If only a single habitat reamins with cubes, the humors move that many steps toward that humor.
If the pawn is on the arm of that humor it takes that many steps toward the edge of the board. If the pawn would go to the edge it sits on the edge of the arm. If the pawn is not on the edge of that humor it rotates in that direction that many steps until it reaches the arm, then marches toward the edge. If the pawn is on the negative edge of that humor it will move toward the center until it crosses to the other side. Note: It is impossible to balance the humors if the pawn is on a red arm of the chart. If the table wishes to balance the humors they must make sure the pawn is on a white arm going into round 4. All cubes return to players and the next round begins.

Turning the Clock

Whenever a point is scored (bird played, food cached, card tucked) turn the clock that many points. Birds only move the clock according to their feather number, birds are not natively worth 1 point (note this is different from the base game).
Eggs and bonus cards do not immediatly move the clock. Eggs/bonus cards are counted and move the clock only at the end of round 4.
All players then collect their 7 cubes, move the invader piece, and begin the next round. Play continues until 4 rounds have passed, after which players determine the end game condition based.
Before balancing the humors, if the invader piece is greater or equal to the player piece the game ends immediatly in an anarchy victory (see below). If the game has not ended move the invader piece up one level and continue the game.

Win Conditions

An ending is determined based on the following chart. Endings with balanced humors generally benefit everyone while unbalanced humors affect only the player with the most eggs.

Anarchy (the clock reaches maximum count)

All birds on player boards are sent to the void. They are not shuffled into the deck in future games.
(starting birds listed on player sheets are not sent to the void)

Circle (semi-balanced humors, clock is not 0)

The player with the most eggs fills in a star on their character sheet for one plus every player in the game. They are encouraged to thank the other players.
(Ex. in a 2 player game the player will fill in 3 stars; 3 player, 4 stars, etc.)

Cooperate (balanced humors, clock is not 0)

Every player fills in a single star on their character sheet. Give yourselves a pat on the back.

Conquor (clock is 0, humors don't matter)

All players sign the difficulty level they beat and fill in two stars on their character sheet.

Tied Egg Count

If the highets number of eggs is tied, the following are tie breakers (in order): Most unused food, total number of birds, most cached food, most tucked cards. If all of those are tied, the oldest player wins ties as a reward for their service to the stars.

Expansions

Players are free to add extra bird packs, but should increase the difficulty based on it. Players more familiar with the expansions are free to tweak numbers to levels that seem appropriate. Conquoring numbers are supposed to be difficult to achieve on a first play, but possible after a few upgrades. Remember players start the game with 5 birds instead of 3 and have a unique player power.
